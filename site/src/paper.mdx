<Section id="method" num={4} kicker="Method" title="Our intuition">

<div class="col method-points">
  <p>We start from <b>rejection sampling</b>: it gives exactly the distribution we want, safe and on-policy.</p>
  <p>Our idea: at every sample, the <b>projection</b> tells us roughly how far we are from the safe set. We use it to <b>bias the next sample</b>.</p>
</div>

<details class="reveal col">
  <summary>Why the projection helps</summary>
  <p>The projected sample tells us where to look. The distance δ between the policy's sample and its projection estimates our uncertainty.</p>
  <div class="two-col">
    <div>
      <h4>Moved a lot (large δ)</h4>
      <p>The projection is less likely to be near an on-policy safe sample: add more noise and search more widely.</p>
    </div>
    <div>
      <h4>Moved a little (small δ)</h4>
      <p>The projection is more likely to be near an on-policy safe sample: add less noise and refine it.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <figure class="fig">
    <div class="on-panel"><Picture src={methodFig} alt="CS-Pi iterations: a large residual renoises deeper (n = 0.6), a smaller residual shallower (n = 0.8)" /></div>
    <figcaption>Large δ₁ renoises deeper (n₁ = 0.6); small δ₂ shallower (n₂ = 0.8).</figcaption>
  </figure>
</details>

<p class="col method-points">This intuition gives the algorithm:</p>

<pre class="algo"><span class="ttl">Algorithm 1  Constraint-Satisfying Flow Policy Sampling</span>{` 1  a₀ ~ 𝒩(0, I);  â⁽⁰⁾ ← `}π<sub>θ</sub>{`(a₀; 0)                `}<span class="c">▷ initial policy draw</span>{`
 2  for i = 0, …, K`}<sub>max</sub>{`−1:
 3      a`}<sub>c</sub>{`⁽ⁱ⁾ ← 𝒫(â⁽ⁱ⁾);  δ⁽ⁱ⁾ ← ‖â⁽ⁱ⁾ − a`}<sub>c</sub>{`⁽ⁱ⁾‖
 4      if δ⁽ⁱ⁾ ≤ τ:  return â⁽ⁱ⁾
 5      n⁽ⁱ⁾ ← k / (k + δ⁽ⁱ⁾);  ε⁽ⁱ⁾ ~ 𝒩(0, I)
 6      a`}<sub>n</sub>{`⁽ⁱ⁾ ← n⁽ⁱ⁾ a`}<sub>c</sub>{`⁽ⁱ⁾ + (1 − n⁽ⁱ⁾) ε⁽ⁱ⁾
 7      â⁽ⁱ⁺¹⁾ ← π`}<sub>θ</sub>{`(a`}<sub>n</sub>{`⁽ⁱ⁾; n⁽ⁱ⁾)              `}<span class="c">▷ re-denoise from time n</span>{`
 8  return 𝒫(â⁽ᴷᵐᵃˣ⁾)                            `}<span class="c">▷ optional terminal projection</span></pre>
